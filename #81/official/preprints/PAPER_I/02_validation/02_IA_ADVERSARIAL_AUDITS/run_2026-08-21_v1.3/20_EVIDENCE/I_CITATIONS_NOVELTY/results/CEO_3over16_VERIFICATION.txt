THE 3/16 CONSTANT — VERIFIED VERBATIM, TWICE
  (a) Abstract, page 21:
      "It is well-known that edge-partitioning a split graph on n vertices may require
       as many as n^2/6 + n/6 cliques. We show that (3/16)n^2 + O(n) cliques will always
       suffice. This also improves a prior result for threshold graphs. In the special
       case where the graph is the difference of cliques, K_n - K_m(bar), at most
       n^2/6 + O(n) cliques are enough."
  (b) PINPOINT, page 23, Section 2 "Statement of results":
      "Theorem 1.  For all split graphs G_n, cp(G_n) <= (3/16) n^2 + O(n)."
      accompanied by "Corollary 1. The same bound applies to threshold graphs."

  VERDICT: the manuscript's attribution of "3n^2/16 + O(n)" for split graphs to
  Chen-Erdos-Ordman is SUPPORTED, with pinpoint Theorem 1, page 23.

TWO FURTHER FINDINGS OF INDEPENDENT VALUE

  1. The same source states, page 22, Example 1:
       "The clique partition number of G_n = K_n - K_2n/3(bar) is n^2/6 + n/6,
        provided 6 divides n."
     This is exactly the complete-split benchmark value that this audit verified
     independently by explicit construction for p = 2..40 in the Paper III Gate F
     evidence (|E| - 2*nu_3 = n^2/6 + n/6). The primary source and the independent
     computation agree.

  2. Section 2, page 23, states:
       "A split graph on n vertices may need n^2/6 + O(n) cliques to partition it.
        We cannot show that this many will suffice. We will show that (3/16)n^2 + O(n)
        will always suffice ..."
     So the authors themselves flag the n^2/6 + O(n) upper bound for GENERAL split
     graphs as open. Their Theorem 2 obtains n^2/6 + O(n) only for the special form
     K_n - K_m(bar) (a difference of cliques), not for general split graphs.
     Consequence for Paper III's novelty positioning: its claimed n^2/6 + O(n) bound for
     ALL split graphs is genuinely stronger than CEO Theorem 1 and is NOT implied by CEO
     Theorem 2. No collision; the improvement 3/16 -> 1/6 is well posed against a gap the
     original authors identified explicitly.
