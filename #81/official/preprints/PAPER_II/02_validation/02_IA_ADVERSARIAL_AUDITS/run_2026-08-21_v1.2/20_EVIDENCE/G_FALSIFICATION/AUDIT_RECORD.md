## Claim under attack (P2-MAIN-V1_2)

From the abstract: for every integer `n >= 1`,

```
max { Phi_tau(G) : G chordal, |V(G)| = n }  =  floor( (2n+1)^2 / 24 )
```

with `Phi_tau(G) = |E(G)| - 2*tau_3^*(G)`, together with the attainment claim that the
maximum is realised on the complete-split family `S_{p,q} = K_p join complement(K_q)`.

This is an **exact equality** claim over a graph class, so it is falsifiable in two
independent ways: a chordal graph exceeding the formula, or no chordal graph attaining it.

## Method

`scripts/chordal_max_exact.py`, written for this audit from the manuscript statement
alone; no author-side script was consulted.

- **Enumeration.** *All* labeled graphs on `n` vertices are generated by iterating over
  every subset of the `C(n,2)` possible edges — a complete enumeration, not a sample.
- **Chordality.** Tested by simplicial-vertex elimination: a graph is chordal iff
  repeated removal of a vertex whose surviving neighbourhood is a clique eliminates every
  vertex. This is exact and needs no clique-tree machinery.
- **The optimum, computed twice.** `nu_3^*` is computed as the fractional
  triangle-*packing* optimum and `tau_3^*` as the fractional triangle-*cover* optimum,
  each by an exact-rational primal simplex written for this audit (Bland's rule, so
  termination is guaranteed and cycling impossible). The manuscript asserts
  `nu_3^* = tau_3^*`; computing both independently turns that assertion into a
  *testable* one rather than an assumption.
- All arithmetic is `fractions.Fraction`. Deterministic; no seeds.

## Results

**The formula is exact on every `n` tested — 0 counterexamples.**

| `n` | max `Phi_tau` found | `floor((2n+1)^2/24)` | Match | Attained by complete-split | Complete-split argmax `(p,q)` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 0 | yes | yes | (0,1), (1,0) |
| 2 | 1 | 1 | yes | yes | (1,1), (2,0) |
| 3 | 2 | 2 | yes | yes | (1,2) |
| 4 | 3 | 3 | yes | yes | (1,3), (2,2) |
| 5 | 5 | 5 | yes | yes | (2,3) |
| 6 | 7 | 7 | yes | yes | (2,4) |

**Attainment (P2-EXTREMIZER).** In every case the global maximum over *all* chordal
graphs is attained by a complete-split graph, independently confirming the manuscript's
reduction to that terminal family.

**Tie structure.** Ties in the maximizing clique size appear exactly at `n = 2`
(`(1,1)` and `(2,0)`) and `n = 4` (`(1,3)` and `(2,2)`); `n = 3, 5, 6` have a unique
complete-split argmax. This is consistent with the manuscript's careful scoping: "The
uniqueness/tie statement concerns the maximizing clique size within the complete-split
family. The paper does not claim that every chordal extremizer is uniquely determined up
to isomorphism." The audit confirms that the weaker claim is the right one — at `n = 6`,
for instance, the global argmax edge set found by enumeration is a complete-split graph,
but the paper does not assert it is the only extremizer up to isomorphism, and this audit
did not test that stronger statement.

**`nu_3^* = tau_3^*`: 0 mismatches** across all 19,048 chordal graphs tested. The
manuscript's identification of the fractional packing and cover optima is independently
confirmed on the whole enumerated domain, computed by two separate linear programs.

## Adversarial notes

- The obvious falsification route — a chordal graph beating the formula — was searched
  exhaustively for `n <= 6` and found nothing. This is a genuine exhaustive result for
  that range, not a sampled one.
- The second route — the formula being unattainable — also fails: attainment holds at
  every `n`, and by a complete-split graph specifically.
- `n = 1` is a real boundary case and the formula handles it: `floor(9/24) = 0`, and the
  single-vertex graph has `Phi_tau = 0`. The manuscript's claimed range `n >= 1` is
  therefore correct at its left endpoint, which is where such formulas usually fail.
- The degenerate case `S_{2,0} = K_2` that the abstract singles out is present in the
  `n = 2` tie and behaves as claimed.

## Gate verdict

`PASS`. The headline exact-maximum claim survived exhaustive falsification over all
19,048 chordal graphs on up to 6 vertices, with the value, the attainment and the
packing/cover identity all independently confirmed in exact arithmetic.

## Limitations

- Exhaustive only to `n = 6` (with `n = 7` attempted). The claim is for all `n >= 1`;
  the manuscript's structural proof is what covers the general case. This gate's role is
  falsification, and it found no counterexample.
- Enumeration is over *labeled* graphs, so isomorphic copies are re-tested. This costs
  time but cannot miss a graph, which is the property that matters here.
- The stronger statement "every chordal extremizer is a complete-split graph up to
  isomorphism" was **not** tested. The manuscript does not claim it.
