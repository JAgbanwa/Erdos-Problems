      Abstract. We prove that the edges of every chordal graph on n vertices
      can be partitioned into n2/6 + O(n) complete subgraphs. This resolves
      a problem of Erds, Ordman, and Zalcstein. In fact, for all sufficiently
      large n, the maximum clique-partition number among n-vertex chordal
      graphs is

                                                n(n + 1)
                                                               .

                                                     6
      The proof combines a weighted fractional packing of triangles and copies
      of K4 with a quantitative stability argument. Near the extremal complete-
      split graphs we construct a partition using only edges and triangles, with
      a strict saving for every structural defect. Far from that family, a
      finite-family packing transfer absorbs its subquadratic rounding error. A
      single-vertex symmetrization path and a first-entry argument connect
      the two regimes.

1. Introduction

   A clique partition of a finite simple graph G is a collection of complete
subgraphs whose edge sets partition E(G). We write cp(G) for the minimum
number of parts in such a partition. Erds, Ordman, and Zalcstein asked
whether every chordal graph on n vertices satisfies

            n2   (1.1)
cp(G)  + O(n).

            6

Their complete-split examples show that the coefficient 1/6 cannot be im-
proved [EOZ93]. The question is listed as Erds Problem 81 [Blo26].

   Recall that a graph is chordal if it contains no induced cycle of length
at least four. Split graphs, whose vertices can be divided into a clique
and an independent set, form an important subclass. Chen, Erds, and
Ordman proved the bound 3n2/16 + O(n) for split graphs [CEO94]. Traverso
determined the exact extremum of a fractional triangle-cover functional over
chordal graphs, with a complete-split extremizer and a clone-copy reduction
[TG26a]. Combined with the standard dense packing transfer, that result
already gives the first-order estimate n2/6+o(n2) for chordal graphs. Traverso

   The proof has two distinct parts. We first introduce a mixed packing

functional in which a triangle has gain 2 and a copy of K4 has gain 5. The

K4 term is structurally useful rather than cosmetic. For the triangle-only
fractional functional, complete graphs remain on the competing n2/6 scale;

allowing K4 with its natural gain   4  - 1 = 5 lowers that branch by a fixed
                                    2

quadratic amount. This isolates the complete-split branch needed by the

stability argument. The corresponding fractional functional is well behaved

under vertex copying. Repeated copies lead monotonically to a complete-

split graph. This gives the correct global quadratic bound, but a general

packing-transfer theorem loses o(n2) and therefore cannot by itself prove

(1.1).

   The second part repairs precisely that loss near the extremal family.

Starting from a clique of order close to n/3, we regularize the choice of root

clique without changing the graph. A balanced Vizing edge-colouring and

the Häggkvist­Janssen list-edge-colouring theorem then produce an integral

triangle packing with a fixed saving for both types of defect: edges outside

the root and missing edges between the root and its complement. This yields

a local stability inequality in the actual labelled edit distance. Finally, a

single-vertex version of the copying argument forces every graph with nearly

extremal fractional value into that local neighbourhood. The first path vertex

entering the neighbourhood would otherwise satisfy incompatible upper and

lower bounds on its edit distance.

            6. A monotone path of single-vertex copies

   The cover-pullback copy inequality, its discrete-convex lift to clone classes,
and the complete-split terminal reduction follow the strategy of Traverso
[TG26a, Sections 3­5] for the triangle-cover functional. We give the mixed-
functional adaptation because both triangle and K4 constraints must be
transported. The additional feature needed later is a fine path recording every
single-vertex copy, so that a first-entry argument can control the moment
the path crosses a fixed edit-distance ball.

   Let u and v be nonadjacent vertices of a graph G. Write Guv for the
graph obtained by deleting the edges incident with u and then making
NGuv (u) = NG(v). In particular, u and v remain nonadjacent.

Lemma 6.1 (copy inequality). For every graph G and every nonadjacent

pair u, v,

                        (Guv) + (Gvu)  2(G).                           (6.1)

Proof. Choose an optimal dual cover y in (2.2). To obtain a cover of Guv,
keep every unaffected edge weight and give each new edge ux the old weight
yvx. Every triangle or K4 containing u is then a copy of one containing v in
G, while no clique contains both u and v. Thus all dual constraints remain
valid.

   Let su and sv be the sums of the y-weights on edges incident with u and
v. The copied covers of Guv and Gvu have respective weights

W4(G) - su + sv and W4(G) - sv + su.

They therefore give

                     W4(Guv) + W4(Gvu)  2W4(G).

   Vertices with the same open neighbourhood are called false twins. Every

false-twin class is independent. Suppose A1 and A2 are distinct false-twin
classes consisting of simplicial vertices, with no edge between the two classes.

Keep the graph outside A1  A2 fixed. If r = |A1| + |A2|, let Hj be the graph
in which j of these r vertices have neighbourhood NG(A1) and the other
r - j have neighbourhood NG(A2). For fixed j, different choices of the j
labelled vertices give isomorphic graphs, via a permutation supported on

A1  A2; hence (Hj) depends only on j. Both neighbourhoods are cliques,
so every Hj is chordal. For 0 < j < r, lemma 6.1 gives

                     (Hj-1) + (Hj+1)  2(Hj).              (6.2)

Thus j  (Hj) is discretely convex. At any interior index, at least one
adjacent value is no smaller. Once such a direction is chosen, the successive
differences in that direction remain nonnegative by (6.2). We may therefore
move one vertex at a time to the corresponding endpoint without ever
decreasing .

   At the endpoint the two selected false-twin classes have merged. No
class outside their union splits: all of its vertices have the same adjacency
to a representative of the target class and consequently acquire the same
adjacency to every copied vertex. The number of false-twin classes has
strictly decreased.

   We next identify the graphs at which this procedure stops; this is the
same terminal family appearing in [TG26a, Section 5].

Lemma 6.2 (terminal characterization). Let G be chordal. If every two
nonadjacent simplicial vertices of G have the same open neighbourhood, then
G is complete-split; that is,

                                       G = Kk  Kn-k

for some 0  k  n.

Proof. Suppose first that G is disconnected and has an edge. Choose a
nonisolated simplicial vertex in a nontrivial component and a simplicial
vertex in another component. Their neighbourhoods are disjoint and at least
one is nonempty, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence a disconnected terminal
graph is edgeless. Complete graphs are also of the required form.

   It remains to consider a connected, noncomplete graph. Take a clique tree
of G with at least two maximal-clique bags. A vertex private to a leaf bag is
12                            ANONYMOUS

simplicial and occurs in no other bag. Private vertices chosen from distinct

leaves are nonadjacent, and hence they have a common open neighbourhood,

say K.

    Each leaf has exactly one private vertex. Otherwise, the neighbourhood

of a private vertex in that leaf would contain another private vertex from

the same leaf, whereas the neighbourhood of a private vertex in a different

leaf would not. It follows that every leaf bag is K together with its unique

private vertex.

    Every member of K occurs in every leaf bag. By the running-intersection

property, it then occurs in every bag, because every node of a finite tree

lies on a path between two leaves. Thus K is complete and universal to

V (G) \ K. Each leaf-private vertex is isolated in G - K.

    If G - K had a nontrivial component, choose a nonisolated simplicial

vertex of that chordal component. Its neighbourhood in G is a clique, so

it is also simplicial in G. It is nonadjacent to a leaf-private vertex, but its

neighbourhood properly contains K, again a contradiction. Therefore G - K

is independent, as required.

              (Gi+1)  (Gi) and |E(Gi+1)  E(Gi)|  n - 2

Proof. If the condition in lemma 6.2 fails, choose two nonadjacent simplicial

                             10. Verification and scope

   The proof invokes three named non-elementary theorem inputs: theo-
rems 2.1 to 2.3. In particular, it does not assume a linear bound for the
general integral­fractional packing gap. The transfer theorem is invoked only
in the far regime, where a fixed quadratic deficit pays for its o(n2) loss. In
the near regime, lemma 3.1 constructs an actual edge partition into triangles
and K2's.

   The accompanying repository contains exact rational checks of the numer-
ical inequalities and finite mixed-packing certificates. These computations
are regression tests; none is a premise of the proof for arbitrary n.

   The Lean companion checks the conditional upper-bound chain: terminal
construction, root regularization, local stability, terminating single-vertex
symmetrization, first entry, and near­far assembly. It also checks the signed-
weight lower bound for complete-split witnesses and combines the two sides
into the eventual exact extremal statement. Its external-input record has
three interfaces corresponding to theorems 2.1 to 2.3; the packing-transfer
interface packages attained rational primal and dual witnesses together with
the published asymptotic gap. These are explicit theorem arguments rather
than project axioms and have not themselves been reproved in Lean.

   The formalization sometimes uses an equivalent proof architecture rather
than mirroring the prose line by line. In particular, its terminal characteriza-
tion uses minimal separators instead of clique trees, and path termination uses
a finite lexicographic measure. The displayed auxiliary bound L  n(n - 1)
is not needed by the final theorem and is not separately formalized. The
rigidity addendum is supported by exact Python certificate replay but lies
outside this Lean theorem perimeter. The repository records the precise
trust ledger and the commands for reproducing the build and the fail-closed
axiom audit.
