Let $G$ be a chordal graph on $n$ vertices - that is, $G$ has no induced cycles of length greater than $3$. Can the edges of $G$ be partitioned into $n^2/6+O(n)$ many cliques? #81 : [Er95] graph theory Asked by Erdős, Ordman, and Zalcstein [EOZ93] , who proved an upper bound of $(1/4-\epsilon)n^2$ many cliques (for some very small $\epsilon>0$). The example of all edges between a complete graph on $n/3$ vertices and an empty graph on $2n/3$ vertices show that $n^2/6+O(n)$ is sometimes necessary. A split graph is one where the vertices can be split into a clique and an independent set. Every split graph is chordal. Chen, Erdős, and Ordman [CEO94] have shown that any split graph can be partitioned into $\frac{3}{16}n^2+O(n)$ many cliques. See also [1017] . Summary: For an \(n\)-vertex chordal graph \(G\), let \(\operatorname{cp}(G)\) be the minimum number of cliques partitioning its edges. The proof combines a weighted fractional packing of triangles and \(K_4\)'s with symmetrization and quantitative stability. Near the complete-split family, edge-colouring methods give an integral partition with savings for structural defects. Far from it, a quadratic deficit absorbs the rounding loss from fractional to integral packings. An explicit construction and a signed-weight argument establish the matching lower bound.
